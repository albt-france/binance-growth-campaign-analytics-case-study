How I approached it (6 steps)

  1. Started from the decision: 'relaunch?' means 'did it create customers worth more than they cost?', not 'was the CAC low?'.
  2. Checked the counting first: deduplicated events, UTC vs UTC+8 day, complete 30-day windows only.
  3. Read the reported numbers (attributed first depositors, CAC) and then the cost the CAC leaves out: promo bonuses.
  4. Looked at the crowd: 30-day retention and the share of depositors who withdrew >= 90% within 30 days.
  5. Measured the incremental sign-ups against untreated regions (difference-in-differences, synthetic control) and set them against the October partner push and the market.
  6. Wrote the decision with its condition: what would have to change for a relaunch to pay back.

3 recommendations

  1. Do not relaunch as is: the CAC is the lowest of the window, but the 30-day ROI is -58% once promo cost is counted and 55% of depositors took >= 90% back out within 30 days.
  2. If relaunched: pay the boosted rate only after a holding period, cap it per user, and judge it on 30-day net deposits and retention, with a withdrawal guardrail written in the plan.
  3. Keep the acquisition partners (the sign-up lift is real); change the offer, not the channel.
